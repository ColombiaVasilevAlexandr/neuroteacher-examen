**Audio Lesson Script: "Naturalization in Colombia"**
**(Duration: approximately 12–14 minutes of reading)**
---
**[Introduction]**
Welcome to our audio lesson dedicated to the naturalization procedure in Colombia. This topic is extremely important for everyone who plans to obtain Colombian citizenship by adoption, that is, not by right of birth, but by decision of the state. Today we will go through the official rules that apply on the basis of information from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Colombia, known as the Cancillería. We will talk about what naturalization is, what requirements are imposed on applicants, how the exam is conducted, and what exceptions exist. We will try to explain everything in simple and clear language so that you can easily navigate this process.
**[Main Part: Definition and Nature of Naturalization]**
Let's start with the most important thing. What is naturalization in Colombia? According to the official reference, naturalization is not merely a formality, but a sovereign and discretionary decision of the President of the Republic. This means that the granting of citizenship is not an automatic right of a foreigner, but a privilege that the state may bestow at its discretion. It is important to understand that this decision is delegated to the Minister of Foreign Affairs. That is, it is the Minister of Foreign Affairs of Colombia who is authorized to sign documents on the granting of citizenship on behalf of the President.
As a result of a successful procedure, the foreigner receives Colombian citizenship by adoption. In Spanish this sounds like "nacionalidad por adopción." Confirmation of this status is a special document called the Carta de Naturaleza. This is a kind of official letter or certificate that legally consolidates your new citizenship.
A key point that must be remembered from the very beginning: naturalization is a discretionary decision. This means that even if you meet all the formal requirements, the state reserves the right of final decision. Therefore, you should not perceive the procedure as a guaranteed transaction, but rather as a process in which you must prove your compliance with all criteria.
**[Main Part: Initial Requirements for the Applicant]**
Before submitting documents, you must make sure that you meet the basic conditions. The first and most important requirement is your place of residence. You must be "domiciliado" in Colombia. This is a legal term that means not merely temporary presence, but a permanent and official place of residence. Simply put, you must permanently reside in the country.
The second mandatory condition is having a valid visa. Moreover, not just any visa, but specifically a Visa de Residente. This visa must be held by you in the status of "titular" or "beneficiario." What does this mean? "Titular" is the main visa holder, that is, you yourself. "Beneficiario" is a person who received the visa as a family member of the main holder, for example, a spouse or child. It is important that the visa be specifically a resident visa, since it confirms your legal status as a permanent resident.
A very important nuance concerns the counting of the residence period. The period of continuous residence in Colombia is counted not from the moment of your first entry into the country, but from the date of issuance of your resident visa. This is an official rule that must be taken into account when planning. For example, if you came to Colombia on a tourist visa and a year later received a resident visa, then your residence period for naturalization will begin only from the moment you receive the resident visa.
It is also worth paying attention to the financial aspect. Payment of the naturalization procedure does not mean automatic granting of citizenship. This is an important warning. Paying the fee only launches the process of reviewing your application, but does not guarantee a positive result. The decision still remains with the state.
**[Main Part: Exam on Knowledge of the Country and Language]**
Now let's move on to the most interesting and important stage — the exam. For those applications for which the documentation has been fully assembled, mandatory confirmation of knowledge is provided. What exactly do you need to know? The list is quite extensive. You will have to demonstrate knowledge in the following disciplines:
1.  **Constitution of Colombia.** This is the fundamental law of the country. You need to know the structure of the state, the rights and duties of citizens, and the foundations of the state system.
2.  **History of Colombia.** Here you will need knowledge of key historical events, important dates, names of outstanding figures, and the main stages of the country's development.
3.  **Geography of Colombia.** You need to be familiar with the administrative divisions, know major cities, rivers, mountains, natural regions, and features of the geographical position.
4.  **Culture of Colombia.** This is a broad concept that includes traditions, customs, holidays, music, literature, and other aspects of cultural heritage.
5.  **Castilian language.** This is the official Spanish language of Colombia. However, there is an important clarification here. The language exam is mandatory only if Spanish is not your native language. If you are a native Spanish speaker, then this point is not an obstacle for you.
How and where does the exam take place? The place and time of its conduct are communicated to the applicant at their email address. It is important that this address be registered in the SITAC system. SITAC is, judging by the context, the electronic system for submitting applications at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Therefore, when registering in this system, make sure that you have provided a current and active email, and check it regularly so as not to miss the exam notification.
**[Main Part: Exceptions to the Rules and Retaking]**
Fortunately, there are categories of citizens who may be exempted from taking the exams. These are official allowances set out in the FAQ (frequently asked questions) of the Ministry.
The first category is applicants who have completed their education in Colombia. If you have completed primary, secondary, or higher education in this country, then you are exempt from the exams. The logic here is simple: it is assumed that during your studies you have already acquired the necessary knowledge about the country, its history, geography, and culture, and also mastered the language at a sufficient level.
The second category is persons over 65 years of age. They are also provided with an exemption from the exams. This is done to make the procedure easier for elderly people, who may find it more difficult to prepare for and take tests.
What should you do if you do not fall into these categories and did not pass the exam on the first attempt? Do not despair. You have the opportunity to retake it. According to the official rules, a retake is possible six months after the initial exam date. This means that you will need to wait six months to try again.
However, simply waiting is not enough. In order to retake the exam, you must officially notify the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of your intention to repeat the exams. This is not an automatic process. You must take the initiative and notify the department of your desire. Only after that will you be admitted to the retake.
**[Explanation and Examples for Better Understanding]**
Let's go through the key points once again to reinforce understanding.
Imagine that you are a foreigner who wants to obtain citizenship. The first thing you do is obtain a resident visa. From that moment, the counting of your continuous residence begins. You live in Colombia, perhaps work or run a business. After a certain time, when you meet all the requirements regarding the residence period, you submit an application through the SITAC system, pay the fee, and provide a complete package of documents.
After your documents are recognized as complete, you are invited to the exam. You receive a notification by email. At the exam, you are tested on the Constitution, history, geography, culture, and possibly language. If you successfully cope, your case is forwarded for review to the Minister of Foreign Affairs, who makes the decision on granting citizenship. In the case of a positive decision, you receive the Carta de Naturaleza.
Now let's consider the situation where you studied at a Colombian university. In this case, you do not need to take the exams. You simply provide your educational documents, and they serve as confirmation of your knowledge. Similarly, if you are over 65 years old, you are also exempt from this procedure.
And what if you failed the exam? Suppose you have poor knowledge of history. You wait six months, and then you write an official statement to the Ministry that you want to retake the exam. After that, you are assigned a new date, and you try again.
**[Conclusion and Brief Recap]**
We have come to the end of our lesson. Let's briefly repeat the main points that we have covered today.
First, naturalization in Colombia is a sovereign and discretionary decision of the President, delegated to the Minister of Foreign Affairs. It is not an automatic right, but a privilege that is formalized through the Carta de Naturaleza.
Second, to begin the procedure, you must be a permanent resident in Colombia ("domiciliado") and have a valid resident visa in the status of titular or beneficiary. The residence period is counted from the date of issuance of this visa. Payment of the procedure does not guarantee the granting of citizenship.
Third, for applicants with a complete package of documents, an exam is mandatory, which tests knowledge of the Constitution, history, geography, culture, and the Castilian language (if it is not native). Information about the place and time of the exam is sent to the email specified in the SITAC system.
Fourth, there are official exceptions. Those who have completed primary, secondary, or higher education in Colombia, as well as persons over 65 years of age, are exempt from the exams.
And finally, fifth, if you did not pass the exam, you can retake it after six months. To do this, you must submit an official statement of intention to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.
We hope that this lesson was useful and helped you structure your knowledge about the naturalization process in Colombia. Remember that the key to success is careful attention to requirements and timely completion of all formalities. We wish you good luck in this important undertaking!
