Audio Lesson: Spanish for the Exam

Introduction

Welcome to the audio lesson on Spanish for preparing for the Colombian citizenship exam. Today we will go over key grammar topics that will help you successfully pass the exam. We will talk about past tenses, the future tense, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, interjections, sentence construction, and stress rules. The lesson is based on materials from the official textbook of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Colombia and the Universidad del Valle.

Part 1. Present Perfect — Pretérito Perfecto

Let's start with the present perfect tense, which in Spanish is called pretérito perfecto. It is used to describe actions that occurred in the past but are connected to the present moment.

How is pretérito perfecto formed?

This tense is built using the present tense of the verb haber and the past participle — participio pasado.

The verb haber in the present tense is conjugated as follows:
- Yo — he
- Tú / vos — has
- Él / ella / usted — ha
- Nosotros / nosotras — hemos
- Vosotros / vosotras — habéis
- Ustedes / ellos / ellas — han

Now let's look at examples of past participles:
- cantar (to sing) → cantado
- ser (to be) → sido
- tomar (to take, to have) → tomado
- escribir (to write) → escrito
- vivir (to live) → vivido
- poner (to put, to place) → puesto
- abrir (to open) → abierto

Note: some participles are irregular, for example, escrito, puesto, abierto. They must be memorized.

When is pretérito perfecto used?

The first meaning is actions within a period of time that is still continuing up to the present moment. Time markers are often used: hoy (today), este año (this year), este mes (this month), esta mañana (this morning), esta tarde (this afternoon), esta semana (this week), últimamente (lately).

Examples:
- Últimamente he ido mucho a cine. — Lately I have gone to the movies a lot.
- Este fin de semana no he salido. — This weekend I haven't gone out.

The second meaning is use with ya (already) and todavía (still/yet):
- ¿Ha llegado ya tu hermana? — Has your sister arrived yet?
- No, no ha llegado todavía. — No, she hasn't arrived yet.

The third meaning is when there is no explicit time marker:
- ¿Has visto mis publicaciones? — Have you seen my posts?

The fourth meaning is for recent news:
- ¿Qué ha pasado? — What happened?
- Ha renunciado el ministro de hacienda. — The Minister of Finance has resigned.

Practical examples of forming forms

Let's practice. Following the model: poner (nosotros) → nosotros hemos puesto.
- Escribir (ella) → ella ha escrito
- Leer (él) → él ha leído
- Lavarse (ellos) → ellos se han lavado
- Levantarse (tú) → tú te has levantado
- Cerrar (nosotros) → nosotros hemos cerrado
- Trabajar (ellas) → ellas han trabajado
- Venir (tú) → tú has venido
- Limpiar (ella) → ella ha limpiado
- Poner (ellos) → ellos han puesto
- Esperar (él) → él ha esperado
- Romper (usted) → usted ha roto

Part 2. Past Imperfect — Pretérito Imperfecto

The next topic is the past imperfect tense, or pretérito imperfecto.

First meaning: habitual actions in the past

This tense indicates what was usual in a certain past period or what a person regularly did during a certain period of life. Expressions often used: cuando era joven (when I was young), en aquella época (in that era), antes (before).

Examples:
- Los Quimbaya se alimentaban de lo que producía el suelo, cazaban y cultivaban maíz, yuca y frijol. — The Quimbaya fed on what the land produced, hunted, and cultivated corn, cassava, and beans.
- Cuando mi marido y yo éramos jóvenes, íbamos al campo todos los fines de semana. — When my husband and I were young, we went to the countryside every weekend.
- Cuando vivía en Bogotá, iba al teatro todas las semanas. — When I lived in Bogotá, I went to the theater every week.

Second meaning: describing people, objects, or places in the past

Example:
- Yo vi al ladrón. Era alto, tenía el pelo corto y llevaba gafas de sol. — I saw the thief. He was tall, had short hair, and was wearing sunglasses.

Forms of regular verbs

Let's look at the conjugation of regular verbs in pretérito imperfecto.

Verb cantar (to sing):
- Yo cantaba
- Tú cantabas
- Él / ella / usted cantaba
- Nosotros / nosotras cantábamos
- Vosotros / vosotras cantabais
- Ellos / ellas / ustedes cantaban

Verb trabajar (to work):
- Yo trabajaba
- Tú trabajabas
- Él / ella / usted trabajaba
- Nosotros / nosotras trabajábamos
- Vosotros / vosotras trabajabais
- Ellos / ellas / ustedes trabajaban

Verb vivir (to live):
- Yo vivía
- Tú vivías
- Él / ella / usted vivía
- Nosotros / nosotras vivíamos
- Vosotros / vosotras vivíais
- Ellos / ellas / ustedes vivían

Irregular verb IR (to go)

The verb ir in pretérito imperfecto is conjugated as follows:
- Yo iba
- Tú ibas
- Él / ella / usted iba
- Nosotros / nosotras íbamos
- Vosotros / vosotras ibais
- Ellos / ellas / ustedes iban

Practical examples

Let's rewrite sentences from the present tense into pretérito imperfecto, describing how things used to be.

Now:
- Me despierto temprano. — I wake up early.
- Trabajo por 8 horas. — I work for 8 hours.
- Me molesta el ruido. — Noise bothers me.
- Solo nado en el verano. — I only swim in the summer.
- Estudio español. — I study Spanish.

When I was young:
- Me levantaba tarde. — I used to get up late.

More examples of composing sentences:
- Mi profesor de música cantaba bonito. — My music teacher sang beautifully.
- Antes comía mucho. — Before, I used to eat a lot.
- No salía porque estaba lloviendo. — I didn't go out because it was raining.
- Me acostaba temprano porque estaba cansada. — I used to go to bed early because I was tired.
- Catalina y mi hermano se peleaban todo el tiempo. — Catalina and my brother fought all the time.
- De pequeña bailaba ella muy bien. — As a child, she danced very well.

Part 3. Simple Future — Futuro Simple

Now let's move on to the simple future tense, which in Spanish is called futuro simple.

When is futuro simple used?

It is used for an action that will happen in the future. Time markers often encountered: luego (later), más tarde (later), el año próximo (next year), el mes que viene (next month), and others.

Examples:
- Mañana cantaré en un bar. — Tomorrow I will sing in a bar.
- A partir del 2022 practicaré la escritura creativa. — Starting from 2022, I will practice creative writing.

How is futuro simple formed?

An important feature: in all three conjugations, the same personal endings are used. Let's look at the table.

Verb trabajar (to work):
- Yo trabajaré
- Tú trabajarás
- Él / ella / usted trabajará
- Nosotros / nosotras trabajaremos
- Vosotros / vosotras trabajaréis
- Ellos / ellas / ustedes trabajarán

Verb comer (to eat):
- Yo comeré
- Tú comerás
- Él / ella / usted comerá
- Nosotros / nosotras comeremos
- Vosotros / vosotras comeréis
- Ellos / ellas / ustedes comerán

Verb vivir (to live):
- Yo viviré
- Tú vivirás
- Él / ella / usted vivirá
- Nosotros / nosotras viviremos
- Vosotros / vosotras viviréis
- Ellos / ellas / ustedes vivirán

Practical examples

Following the model: trabajas → trabajarás (trabajar).
- Llueve → lloverá (llover)
- Miran → mirarán (mirar)
- Envío → enviaré (enviar)
- Cogen → cogerán (coger)
- Olvidan → olvidarán (olvidar)
- Empiezo → empezaré (empezar)
- Quieres → querrás (querer)

Part 4. Adverbs — Los Adverbios

Now let's talk about adverbs. Adverbs modify the meaning of a verb, adjective, or another adverb. They provide circumstantial information about time, place, quantity, manner, doubt, or affirmation.

Examples of adverbs: ahí (there), aquí (here), lejos (far), cerca (near), bien (well), despacio (slowly), rápido (quickly), mucho (a lot), muy (very), acaso (perhaps), mientras (while), obligatoriamente (obligatorily).

Let's look at examples of usage:
- La construcción de la obra necesita, obligatoriamente, un permiso. — The construction of the project obligatorily requires a permit. Here the adverb obligatoriamente modifies the verb necesita.
- Esa compra le salió muy cara. — That purchase turned out very expensive for him/her. Here the adverb muy intensifies the adjective cara.
- Otra vez llegó bien tarde. — He/she arrived very late again. Here the adverb bien modifies another adverb — tarde.

How to find adverbs in sentences?

Let's look at examples:
- Carlos llegó rápido y tuvo que esperar fuera. — Carlos arrived quickly and had to wait outside. Adverbs: rápido, fuera.
- Ese es el colegio donde estudié. — That is the school where I studied. Adverb: donde.
- El carro apareció de repente. — The car appeared suddenly. Adverb: de repente.
- Encontré los documentos dentro del cajón. — I found the documents inside the drawer. Adverb: dentro.
- Sus notas mejoraron bastante en comparación al mes anterior. — His/her grades improved considerably compared to the previous month. Adverb: bastante.

Supplementing sentences with adverbs

Here is a list of adverbs for practice: tarde (late), fuera (outside), delante (in front), dónde (where), claro (clearly), muy (very), extremadamente (extremely).

Examples:
- Alicia vendrá tarde al colegio. — Alicia will come to school late.
- Los estudiantes se quedaron fuera de clase. — The students stayed outside the class.
- ¿Dónde está la oficina del Sr. Martínez? — Where is Mr. Martínez's office?
- Los niños se sientan delante de los adultos. — The children sit in front of the adults.
- Claro que quiero continuar jugando. — Of course I want to continue playing.
- Tu madre es una persona muy agradable. — Your mother is a very pleasant person.
- Ese hombre es extremadamente organizado. — That man is extremely organized.

Part 5. Prepositions — Las Preposiciones

Prepositions establish a relationship or connection between two or more words or elements of a sentence.

The textbook provides the following list of Spanish prepositions: a, ante, bajo, cabe, con, contra, de, desde, durante, en, entre, hacia, hasta, mediante, para, por, según, sin, so, sobre, tras, versus, vía.

Let's look at examples of preposition usage:
- Andrés viajó a Bogotá. — Andrés traveled to Bogotá. The preposition a indicates direction.
- En Buenaventura las ballenas aparecen ante nosotros repentinamente. — In Buenaventura, whales suddenly appear before us. The preposition ante means "before."
- Los zapatos están bajo la cama. — The shoes are under the bed. The preposition bajo means "under."
- La carne viene con papa salada y plátano maduro. — The meat comes with salted potatoes and ripe plantain. The preposition con means "with."
- Colombia jugó contra Brasil en las eliminatorias. — Colombia played against Brazil in the qualifiers. The preposition contra means "against."
- La mesa es de madera. — The table is made of wood. The preposition de indicates material.
- No veo a mi esposa desde la mañana. — I haven't seen my wife since morning. The preposition desde means "since."
