Territory and Climate of Colombia

Let us begin with the climate, since it has a strong influence on the water cycle and vegetation. The climatic features of Colombia are determined primarily by the country's geographical and astronomical position, as well as by its relief.

According to many geographers, Colombia occupies an exceptionally advantageous position, thanks to which it is distinguished by enormous climatic diversity. The country is located at the far north of South America, between the vast forests of Amazonia, Panama, and the Pacific and Atlantic Oceans, which have a significant influence on the climate. Among the main factors that most accurately characterize the climate of the region are precipitation, temperature, and winds.

Given the country's geographical position, the temperature during the year should vary only slightly. However, differences in elevation above sea level lead to regional temperature variations and to the formation of so-called altitudinal thermal belts.

So what are altitudinal thermal belts? These are zones that form depending on elevation above sea level. In Colombia, several such belts are distinguished.

The first is the hot belt. These are territories below 1000 meters above sea level. Here the average temperature is above 24 degrees Celsius.

The second is the temperate belt. It is located at an elevation of 1000 to 2000 meters. The average temperature here is about 18 degrees Celsius.

The third is the cold belt. It is located at an elevation of 2000 to 3000 meters. The temperature here is not below approximately 10 degrees Celsius.

The fourth is the páramo belt. These are territories above 3000 meters. The average temperature here is below 10 degrees Celsius.

And finally, above 4700 meters are the zones of perpetual snow.

It is important to note that between neighboring altitudinal belts there is a transitional strip about 400 meters wide.

According to climate research data, the temperatures recorded in Colombia in the period from 1981 to 2008 differ significantly. The main reason is the great diversity of elevations above sea level across the country's territory.

The elevation of Colombia's territory varies from sea level to more than 5000 meters in the Andes and the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta. There are the country's highest peaks — Cristóbal Colón and Simón Bolívar, whose height, according to later measurements, is about 5700 meters above sea level.

Hydrography of Colombia

Now let us move on to hydrography. Water is one of the most widespread elements of nature. Constantly moving, it is a powerful factor in shaping the earth's surface: it creates mountain forms and canyons, forms basins and valleys, transports and deposits nutrients and sediments.

Drainage Slopes

Drainage slopes are territories into which the waters of river basins flow. In Colombia, five main drainage directions are distinguished: Caribbean, Pacific, Amazon, Orinoco, and Catatumbo.

The Caribbean slope has an area of about 389,000 square kilometers. It includes the basins of the Magdalena and Cauca rivers. The Magdalena is one of the country's most important rivers; its length is 1540 kilometers. The Cauca has a length of 1250 kilometers. Thanks to their length, these rivers are among the most important in the country; about 70 percent of the population lives on their banks. Along their course, the rivers cross various altitudinal belts and ecosystems: páramo, humid and dry forests, humid tropical forests, swampy areas, and arid zones. This same slope also includes the basins of the Atrato and Sinú rivers, the basin of the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta, and La Guajira.

The Pacific slope includes the basins of the Patía, San Juan, Mira, Baudó rivers and other smaller tributaries. Its area is about 88,000 square kilometers. The rivers are characterized by rapid flow; high humidity and rich vegetation create conditions for significant biodiversity.

The Orinoco slope is formed by the Guaviare, Meta, Vichada, Tomo, and Arauca rivers. Its area is about 328,000 square kilometers. This is also a zone of high biodiversity.

The Amazon slope has an area of about 332,000 square kilometers. It includes the basins of the “white” rivers Caquetá, Putumayo, Guayabero-Guaviare and the “black” rivers Inírida, Apaporis, Mirití-Paraná, and Yarí. A large number of species of fish, birds, amphibians, and reptiles live here.

The Catatumbo slope is the smallest; its area is about 18,700 square kilometers. It includes the Catatumbo, Zulia, and Sardinata rivers.

River Basins

River basins are geographical and hydrological systems formed by a main river and the entire territory participating in runoff from the river's source to its mouth.

Hydrographic areas correspond to major hydrographic regions or drainage slopes — large basins uniting rivers and their tributaries that flow into the same sea. In Colombia, four main runoff directions are distinguished: two are connected with rivers of continental significance — the Orinoco and the Amazon — and the other two with the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans.

In addition, the Magdalena-Cauca hydrographic area is distinguished. Although it belongs to the Atlantic runoff, this basin has special socio-economic significance because of its high population density and substantial contribution to the country's gross domestic product.

Relief of Colombia

Now let us consider the relief. The territory of Colombia consists of approximately 33 percent mountains and 67 percent lowland plains. Three main sectors can be distinguished in its relief.

The first sector is the Andean mountain system, formed by three Cordilleras and intermontane valleys. It occupies the western part of the country.

The second sector is the vast lowland plains in the east, in the Orinoquía and Amazonía regions, as well as the plains of the Pacific and Caribbean coasts, respectively in the west and north of the country.

The third sector is the peripheral mountain system, including isolated mountain massifs, for example the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta and the Serranía de la Macarena.

Colombia's mountain system is part of the great Andes chain, which begins in Argentina and ends in Venezuela. The Andes enter Colombian territory from the south, at the border with Ecuador, in the area of the Huaca massif, better known as the Nudo de los Pastos. Here are located the high mountain plateaus of Ipiales, Túquerres, and Pasto, as well as numerous volcanoes — Cumbal, Azufral, and Galeras.

Let us consider the three Cordilleras that cross Colombian territory: the Western, Central, and Eastern.

Western Cordillera

The Western Cordillera begins in the Nudo de los Pastos and stretches along the Pacific coast to the Paramillo node. Here it divides, forming the Abibe range in the west, San Jerónimo in the center, and Ayapel in the east. The length of the Cordillera is about 1200 kilometers, the area is about 76,000 square kilometers, and the average elevation is about 2000 meters above sea level.

Central Cordillera

The Central Cordillera extends from the Nudo de los Pastos to the Caribbean plain. Its length is about 1000 kilometers, its area is 110,000 square kilometers, and its average elevation is about 3000 meters above sea level, while individual peaks exceed 5000 meters. The Cordillera is located between the valleys of the Cauca River in the west and the Magdalena River in the east.

Eastern Cordillera

The Eastern Cordillera is the longest of the three Cordilleras: about 1200 kilometers in length and 130,000 square kilometers in area. Geologically it is the youngest and at the same time the widest. It begins in the Colombian massif, and in the far north, at the Santurbán node, it divides and gives rise to the Cordillera de Mérida in Venezuela, as well as the Motilones and Perijá ranges in Colombia.

Population of Colombia

Now let us talk about the population. The Political Constitution of 1991 defined the following territorial entities of the country: departments, districts, municipalities, and indigenous territories. This is precisely how the country's territory is organized, and consequently its population as well.

Within such spatial and administrative division, there are 1023 municipalities, 19 departmental corregimientos, and 8406 populated settlements.

Colombia is distinguished by pronounced regional diversity associated with its natural regions: Caribbean, Amazon, Orinoco, Andean, and Pacific.

The regional division most often used in Colombia was developed by the Regional Council for Economic and Social Policy of the National Planning Department and unites 32 departments into six regions.

The Atlantic region includes the departments of Atlántico, Bolívar, Cesar, Córdoba, La Guajira, Magdalena, and Sucre.

The Eastern region is Boyacá, Cundinamarca, Meta, Norte de Santander, and Santander.

Bogotá, the Capital District, is distinguished separately.

The Central region includes Antioquia, Caldas, Caquetá, Huila, Quindío, Risaralda, and Tolima.

The Pacific region is Cauca, Chocó, Nariño, and Valle del Cauca.

The National Territories include Amazonas, Arauca, Casanare, Guainía, Guaviare, Putumayo, San Andrés y Providencia, Vaupés, and Vichada.

Migration Flows

Colombia was not a country that received large migration flows from other states. As a rule, these were small groups that created relatively closed colonies. Some of them had a noticeable influence on medium-sized cities and especially on the development of entrepreneurship in the country.

Among the most notable migration flows of the 20th century is Arab migration from countries such as Lebanon, Syria, and Jordan. The settlers settled mainly in the north of the country, for example in Maicao. It is there that the largest Muslim community in Colombia and the second-largest mosque in Latin America are located.

Jews from Poland, Ukraine, Lithuania, and Germany also arrived in Colombia.

Historical Features and Cultural Traits

Historical features and cultural traits are connected with various ways of life and worldviews that do not necessarily coincide with the customs of the majority of the population in matters of ethnicity, religion, language, economy, and political organization.

Indigenous ethnic groups make up 3.4 percent of the country's population. They live predominantly in rural areas and are most widely represented in the departments of Cauca, Nariño, La Guajira, Putumayo, and Amazonas.

As for the population of African origin, according to the 2005 census, 4,533,951 people indicated their belonging to Afro-Colombians, Raizales, and Palenqueros. This corresponded to 10.6 percent of the country's total population. Most of this population lives in cities; the most significant concentration is noted in Cali, Cartagena, Buenaventura, Barranquilla, and, to a lesser extent, Medellín. The Pacific region is the main area of settlement of the Afro-Colombian population. Its cultural features are especially noticeable in speech, women's oral traditions, music, dances, and attitude toward their territory.
