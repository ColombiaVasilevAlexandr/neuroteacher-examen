Audio Lesson: “The Constitution and the State of Colombia”

Introduction

Hello! Today we begin studying a topic of the utmost importance for preparing for the Colombian citizenship exam — the Political Constitution and the structure of the state. This lesson will help you understand the foundations of Colombia’s constitutional order, the principles organizing public authority, and the mechanisms for protecting citizens’ rights.

We will examine five main sections: the foundational principles and rights in the Constitution, the mechanisms for protecting rights, democratic culture and civic participation, the organization of state institutions, and the formation of civic ethics and a culture of peace.

Part 1. Foundational Principles and Rights in the Constitution

The Preamble

Let us begin with the Preamble to the 1991 Constitution. The Preamble is the introductory part of the Constitution that reflects the political and legal meaning that the people of Colombia gave to the fundamental law. It sets out the guiding principles and goals toward which the constitutional order is directed.

It is important to understand that the Preamble is not merely a formal part of the text. It has binding significance for legislative, administrative, and judicial activity. The Preamble serves as a guide when reviewing the constitutionality of laws and other legal acts. Political and legal provisions of this level must influence the exercise of state power and direct it toward the goals enshrined in the Preamble.

The Social Rule-of-Law State

A key principle of the Colombian Constitution is the social rule-of-law state. What does this mean? First of all, this principle limits the power of the state so that it cannot threaten citizens’ rights and freedoms. At the same time, the state is obliged to fulfill certain social goals, and therefore to participate actively in public life.

The word “social” means that state activity must ensure decent living conditions for people. The state’s task is not limited to non-interference in freedoms: it must also counteract social inequality and create for everyone real opportunities to develop their abilities and overcome material hardship.

Decentralization

Article 1 of the Constitution establishes a decentralized model of political organization and the autonomy of territorial entities. Decentralization is a method of governance in which functions and powers are transferred from the central authority to territorial entities for more independent exercise. Its goal is to increase the effectiveness of public administration.

The Constitution defines the powers of territorial entities, the competence of Congress to regulate the degree of their autonomy, and the principles of coordination between the central authority and territorial bodies.

Popular Sovereignty

In a modern state, power is based on the principle of popular sovereignty. The people, exercising their original constituent power, adopt the Constitution, through which they determine the form of the state, the system of government, and the political regime.

Colombia’s Constitutional Court emphasizes that the people’s original constituent power is not limited by ordinary legal frameworks and represents a full exercise of citizens’ political power. Only the people have the right to adopt the Constitution or replace it. The Constitution is the legal expression of the social contract that gives the state legitimacy.

Supremacy of the Constitution

The principle of supremacy is based on the normative nature of the Constitution as the primary source of the legal order. Article 4 of the Political Constitution establishes: “The Constitution is the norm of norms. Where the Constitution is incompatible with a law or another legal norm, the constitutional provisions shall apply.”

The entire remaining legal system is subordinate to the Constitution. Constitutional norms are the starting foundation of domestic law. The Constitutional Court defines the Constitution as the highest and final framework within which the membership of any norm in the legal system and its legal validity are assessed.

Inalienable Rights

The concept of inalienable rights is based on the natural-law approach: rights belong to a person by virtue of their very nature and therefore are subject to protection. At the same time, they serve as a limitation on state power.

An inalienable right cannot be alienated or turned into an object of transaction. Basic rights exist before the state and do not depend on its recognition. The fundamental human rights provided for by the 1991 Constitution belong to every person by virtue of their human dignity.

Fundamental rights are considered inherent in a person from the moment of their existence — in the wording of the textbook, even from the moment of conception — and precede the state. Life, dignity, private life, and freedom are classified as fundamental rights because of their inalienable character.

Protection of the Family

The Constitutional Court defines the family as a community of people linked by natural or legal ties, founded on love, respect, and solidarity. The family is characterized by a unity of life that closely binds its members.

The Colombian constitutional order seeks to make the family a space where, under conditions of respect, non-violence, and equality, each of its members can develop freely and fully. It is necessary to maintain a balance between the stability of the family, dignity, and the free development of the personality of each of its members.

Recognition of Ethnic and Cultural Diversity

The recognition of ethnic and cultural diversity is consistent with the constitutional norms on the preservation, protection, and restoration of the environment and natural resources. The textbook emphasizes the special connection of indigenous communities with territories and ecosystems of exceptional natural and cultural value.

The indigenous population and the natural environment are regarded as an interconnected system deserving comprehensive state protection. Recognition of such diversity presupposes real and effective guarantee of the fundamental rights of members of indigenous communities.

Protection of National Sovereignty and Self-Determination

The constitutional requirement to build international relations on national sovereignty, self-determination, and recognition of the principles of international law is of particular importance. At the same time, the modern understanding of sovereignty can no longer fully correspond to the strict model of classical nineteenth-century constitutional theory.

Economic and cultural interdependence and the emergence of problems that can be solved only at the global level make a more flexible understanding of sovereignty necessary. Such a model must preserve the essential space of state freedom and self-determination without denying the norms and principles that have received universal recognition.

Rights, Guarantees, and Duties

The Colombian Constitution establishes three generations of rights.

**Individual rights, or first-generation rights.** Any democratic society has an interest in respect for the individual rights of every citizen. This concerns the quality of democracy and the protection of basic individual freedoms. For example, when the state recognizes a person’s right to conscientious objection, it thereby strengthens a fundamental value important to society as a whole.

**Economic, social, and cultural rights, or second-generation rights.** The Colombian Constitution broadly establishes social, economic, and cultural rights. On the one hand, these are full constitutional rights with normative force. On the other, many of them are formulated as programmatic provisions that acquire more concrete legal content after being developed by the legislature. Most such rights presuppose the provision of certain goods or services by the state and public expenditure.

**Collective rights, or third-generation rights.** The Constitution classifies as collective rights: public property, public space, public safety and sanitary well-being, the integrity of public administration, the environment, and free economic competition. This list is not closed.

A collective right relates to the interest of a group of people, and not exclusively to a private subjective benefit. At the same time, any member of the relevant community may go to court to protect the collective, while also protecting their own interest.

Guarantees

The person and their dignity are the starting foundation and key element of the social rule-of-law state. The state is evaluated by its ability to protect freedom, promote equality, and ensure the real exercise of rights by all members of society.

A just order exists not when rights and duties are merely proclaimed, but when they are actually embodied in life. This corresponds to one of the main goals of the state enshrined in Article 2 of the Constitution.

Duties

Personal autonomy is part of the protection of human dignity. Freedom to choose a particular life path in the conditions of society is a manifestation of human dignity. Such freedom presupposes the greatest possible independence with the minimum necessary restrictions.

State bodies and private persons must refrain from prohibitions and unjustified obstruction of a person’s real life self-determination when the necessary social conditions for their full development exist.

Part 2. Mechanisms for Protecting Rights

Now let us examine the mechanisms for protecting rights that the Colombian Constitution provides.

Acción de tutela

The first and most important mechanism is acción de tutela. This is a special judicial mechanism that any person may use for the immediate protection of their fundamental rights if they are violated or under threat as a result of an action or omission of a state body or a private person.

This remedy has a subsidiary and residual character: as a rule, it is used when the injured party has no other judicial means of protection. If another means exists, tutela may still be used temporarily to prevent irreparable harm.

The main characteristics of tutela: subsidiarity, immediacy, simplicity, specific focus, and effectiveness. The procedure is based on the principles of minimal formalism and the active role of the court.

Acción de cumplimiento

The second mechanism is acción de cumplimiento. It allows a person to go to court to secure from an authority the performance of a duty expressly established by law or an administrative act. To file, there is no need to prove a special personal interest.

The main condition is the absence of another effective judicial remedy. The mechanism may be applied to a state body or to a private person performing public functions. Acción de cumplimiento is aimed at ensuring that laws and administrative acts operate in reality, not merely formally.

Acción popular

The third mechanism is acción popular. This is a procedural mechanism for the effective judicial protection of collective rights and the interests of a group of persons. Its goals may be preventive — to prevent possible harm; restraining — to stop a danger or an ongoing violation; restorative — to return the situation to its previous state.

Any person may initiate such an action. It may be directed against state bodies or private persons, pursues a public goal, and is not directly intended to obtain monetary compensation.

Acción de grupo

The fourth mechanism is acción de grupo. This is a judicial mechanism by which a group of persons seeks compensation for harm caused to a collective interest or to the individual rights of members of the group by actions or omissions of the state or private persons, if the harm has a common cause.

Unlike acción popular, here it is necessary to prove the concrete harm for which compensation is sought. The subject of the claim is the personal property interest of each member of the group.

Habeas corpus

The fifth mechanism is habeas corpus. It is simultaneously a fundamental right and a constitutional remedy for the protection of personal freedom. It applies if a person is deprived of liberty in violation of constitutional or legal guarantees, or if the deprivation of liberty is unlawfully continuing.

In its consideration, the pro homine principle applies — preference for the interpretation of law that best protects the person. Habeas corpus is closely linked to the protection of life and personal integrity, and in cases of arbitrary detention it serves as a guarantee against enforced disappearance.

Habeas data

The sixth mechanism is habeas data. This is an independent fundamental right that includes a person’s right to access their data, to request the inclusion or exclusion of data, to correct, supplement, and update it, and to restrict the dissemination of personal data.

The right applies to the management of both public and private personal data databases. Organizations that store personal information are obliged to properly administer and protect the relevant archives and databases.

Derecho de petición

The seventh mechanism is the right of petition, derecho de petición. This is the fundamental right of every person to send respectful statements and requests to state bodies, as well as to private persons performing public functions, and to receive a timely and substantive response.

The response need not be favorable to the petitioner, but it must genuinely address the factual and legal grounds of the question raised. Evasive and abstract responses do not meet the requirements of Article 23 of the Constitution.

Part 3. Democratic Culture and Civic Participation

Civic participation is understood as a social process arising from the coordinated actions of people and groups who pursue certain goals and express different interests.

Participation is connected simultaneously with the rights and duties of citizens. As a right, it means a person’s opportunity to intervene in the adoption of important decisions and to be heard. As a duty, participation is expressed through public oversight, which makes it possible to prevent problems, propose solutions, and monitor and evaluate governance.

The Constitutional Court regards civic participation as one of the fundamental principles of the social rule-of-law state. Democratic participation is a right-duty: simultaneously an opportunity and a responsibility to be present in public life.

Mechanisms of Civic and Political Participation

Forms of direct political participation include the opportunity for citizens to run for elective office themselves. Forms of indirect participation include electing representatives and using the mechanisms listed in Article 103 of the Constitution.
